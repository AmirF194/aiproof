Limitations

AIProof is an analytical framework, not a forecast. The scores are designed to help you think about role durability — they are not a guarantee of which jobs will or won't exist in 10 years. Before you act on anything you see here, please read the limitations below.

1. Scores are estimates, not measurements

Two of the four base axes (demand, salary) have public data behind them. The other two (automation resistance, strategic importance) and all four derived dimensions are calibrated qualitative estimates encoded as numbers on a 0–10 scale. Treat them as informed opinion, not laboratory measurement.

2. Labour markets shift

Hiring patterns, layoffs, immigration policy, and macroeconomic conditions move independently of AI capability. A role that scores well today can have its labour market reshaped by factors completely outside this methodology.

3. AI capability changes quickly

The score reflects 2026 tooling. A single foundation-model breakthrough or a sustained capability plateau will both meaningfully change the ranking. We re-evaluate yearly; in practice the rubric should be re-run, not enshrined.

4. Role titles vary across companies

"AI Engineer" means at least four different jobs depending on the employer. We score the underlying work as best we can identify it, but title noise is a real source of error. If your job's day-to-day looks materially different from what a category implies, weight the category-average less and your own role-specific evidence more.

5. Geographic, seniority, and industry bias

  • Geography: US/EU bias. Mobile, Frontend, and QA Automation hold up longer in India, LATAM, and SEA markets than these scores suggest.
  • Seniority: The framing is senior-IC. Junior dynamics are harsher than scores indicate — AI compresses the bottom of every ladder hardest.
  • Industry: Tech-product / FAANG-adjacent assumed. Defense, healthcare, and finance shift the rankings — Mobile and QA Manual hold longer in regulated industries because of compliance overhead.

6. Live data has uneven coverage

Roughly 141 of 1,000 roles currently have a live posting count this week from our crawlers. The other ~860 use the calibrated baseline only. We surface this honestly via the confidence score on every role page — a score of 80+ means the role has live data behind it; below 60 means coverage gaps you should be aware of.

7. The score is not

  • A salary predictor. Compensation is correlated with score but not determined by it. Employer, geography, and timing matter more.
  • A fit assessment. The safest career you hate is still a bad career.
  • Personalised advice. Use this as one input alongside conversations with people in the role, mentors, and your own evidence.
